# CO₂ Emissions & Renewable Energy Analysis

Exploratory data analysis of the relationship between renewable energy adoption and global CO₂ emissions between 2020 and 2024, built as a class project for Talento Tech 2.

## Objective

To assess whether the increase in the use of renewable energy has contributed to the reduction of global CO₂ emissions.

## Research Questions

- How have global CO₂ emissions changed over time?
- Which countries show the highest levels of CO₂ emissions?
- How have emissions evolved among the leading emitting countries?
- Which countries have recorded the largest increases or reductions in their CO₂ emissions?
- How has the use of renewable energy evolved worldwide?
- Which countries show the largest share of renewable energy?
- Is there an observable relationship between increased use of renewable energy and reduced CO₂ emissions?

## Methodology

1. Data import and cleaning (missing values, duplicates, inconsistencies)
2. Integration of emissions and renewable energy datasets
3. Exploratory data analysis
4. Visualizations (trends, country comparisons, correlation matrix, heatmap)
5. Correlation analysis
6. Interpretation of results and conclusions

## Tools

- Python
- Pandas / NumPy
- Matplotlib / Seaborn / Plotly
- Statsmodels / SciPy
- Jupyter Notebook / Google Colab

## Key Finding

No strong linear relationship was found between renewable energy share and CO₂ emissions (correlation ≈ -0.02). While renewable energy share grew steadily (from ~10% in 2020 to ~22% in 2024), global CO₂ emissions did not show a sustained decline over the same period. Results should be treated as exploratory, not causal.
